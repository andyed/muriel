# How Muriel’s jury reviews a design

A reviewer who sees everything can miss a defect by supplying the context that
makes it seem reasonable. Muriel’s jury gives each reviewer a different, limited
view. The Squinter gets a blur. The Stranger gets one look without the brief.
The Pedant gets the text. Their findings can disagree, and that disagreement
is part of the result.

## One case, different evidence

The illustration records **case jc-1024-x7**, a review of Muriel’s own logo card.
Five jurors took part. The sixth lens, Loupe, is illustrated but sat this case
out. The quotations below come from the five participating jurors’ ballots.

### Squinter: the blurred composition

Sees a heavy blur, without readable text. Looks for hierarchy and competing
visual masses; does not judge typographic craft.

> “At mid-distance the mark reads as two blocks, a bowtie.”

### Stranger: one look, no brief

Sees the sharp artifact once, without an explanation of what it should mean.
Reports what it communicates on first encounter.

> “I could not work out what ‘live constraint system’ means.”

### Pedant: the words and numbers

Sees the text and checks labels, units, and states. Does not judge composition.

> “The only state word on the canvas is ‘live’.”

### Thumbnail: the small version

Sees the artifact at one-eighth scale and 16 pixels wide. Reports what survives
and what merges or disappears; does not judge the full-size layout.

> “Mark plus wordmark read as one T-shaped blob.”

### Forger: an attempted imitation

Builds a counterfeit to test where the artifact’s distinctiveness lies.
Its own render is shown in the illustration.

> “All distinctiveness sits in one element.”

### Loupe: the close-up

Sees magnified crops, without the whole composition. Looks for gaps, joints,
collisions, clipping, and unfinished repetition. The illustrated crop shows
a three-pixel offset at the artifact’s original scale. This is a measured
example of the lens, **not a ballot from this case**.

## What the case found

Before the jury met, a pixel measurement found that the logo card’s second
tagline had **6.51:1 contrast**, below Muriel’s 8:1 text target. A vote cannot
overrule that result. The case retained the original card as its exhibit;
the illustration is a critique of that card, not a claim that it passed.

The most severe juror finding came from the Pedant: the word “live” did not
explain that the image was a still from a live system. The Forger found that
nearly all of the design outside the mark could be copied from a template.
The Thumbnail found that small copy dissolved at link-preview sizes.
The Squinter and Stranger reported no formal findings, even though their
observations helped describe the artifact.

## How to use the jury

Use a panel when a consequential visual decision remains contested after
measurable checks. Choose the lenses that address the decision. Keep ballots
sealed until all participating reviewers have responded, and preserve the
findings rather than averaging away a serious defect.

These are AI reviews of an artifact. They can help find problems and compare
directions; they do not establish how people will understand or use the result.

The [full protocol](../references/jury.md) covers seat selection, isolated
ballots, disagreements, and the decision record. For a straightforward review,
start with the [critique agent](../agents/muriel-critique.md).
