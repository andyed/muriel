    HyperFrames films¶

    Watch both films in the showcase · Browse all examples · Read the video guide

    These films use the same material as the interactive examples: a collection, its spatial arrangements, and the detail within each item. The app provides the footage. HyperFrames composes the sequence, transitions, and typography. The previews preserve the existing edits at 1280 × 720, 30 fps; playback is manual, with native video controls and no automatic looping.

    Cats. Lots of cats.¶

    Watch the 28-second film · Collection guide · Wunderkammer source

    A silent tour of 489 cat images through Wunderkammer. The artwork was generated with Adobe Firefly; this is an illustrative collection, not a historical art dataset. Era and style labels come from the generation prompts.

    What happens on screen¶

    Time Scene
    0–2.7 s A single cat in the detail view. “Cats.” appears, with the Firefly provenance label.
    2.7–13.3 s A flash opens into rows of cat images rushing into an aisle. “Lots of cats.” becomes “Walk the aisle” as the camera travels through the shelves.
    13.3–16.7 s The wall holds, then filters to ginger coats.
    16.7–19.2 s Images float in space, placed by colour.
    19.2–21.9 s An axes view places era horizontally and lightness vertically.
    21.9–24.8 s A comparison connects an observation to its cited images.
    24.8–27.8 s The Wunderkammer end card names the collection explorer, repository, and MIT licence for the software.

    How it was made¶

    The aisle flight is captured frame by frame from Wunderkammer, with camera and rack positions set for each frame. Repeated racks extend the hallway for the long cut. Detail, filtering, axes, and comparison footage come from the running interface; the colour-space dolly is also captured frame by frame.

    HyperFrames assembles the shots, dark caption panels, and flashes through white. Keeping the panels separate from the captured interface makes their timing and type editable without recapturing the underlying view. The film has no audio.

    Psychodeli spatial reel¶

    Watch the 39-second film · Spatial guide · Explore Psychodeli

    Recorded Psychodeli visuals play on tiles arranged with Wunderkammer’s spatial machinery. This is an authored camera sequence through those arrangements. The film contains music and a chant texture, with no spoken instructions.

    What happens on screen¶

    Time Scene
    0–10 s Shelves of moving visuals rush into a warehouse aisle; the camera travels between them.
    10–14 s A swoop through stacks of video tiles.
    14–20.57 s The camera skims along a wall of moving images.
    20.57–33.87 s Cards lift off the wall into a sphere around a central sequence of Psychodeli visuals. A brand card turns into view.
    33.87–39.07 s The Psychodeli wordmark lands with “A dance partner for your senses.” The end card includes the website, platform call to action, and QR code.

    How it was made¶

    Captured warehouse, stacks, wall, and sphere shots become video layers in a HyperFrames composition. The wordmark’s structure stays fixed while light moves through its bands. The cuts and brand reveal follow changes in the soundtrack: the chant’s return, a quieter passage for the card turn, and a surge for the logo.

    The visuals are Psychodeli recordings. The soundtrack is Andy’s Adobe Firefly generation, edited from “Track 4 — Buddhist monk chanting trance dub — echoes, stereo spins, get trippy.” There is no added voiceover.

    Use the approach¶

    1. Capture the product’s actual interaction or rendering before adding titles.
    2. Keep shots, caption timing, and brand motion in the composition.
    3. Inspect the rendered text and transitions at the intended viewing size.
    4. Keep the final film beside its interactive example and explain which parts were captured and which were authored.

    The video channel covers the production workflow; motion craft covers staging and continuity. The media manifest records the delivery copies, durations, byte sizes, and checksums used here.
