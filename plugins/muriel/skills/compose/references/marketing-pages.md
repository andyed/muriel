# Marketing pages — visitor context, claims, and evidence

Load for a landing page or campaign whose audience, argument, or intended next
action needs design work. This is optional guidance alongside the selected
web, polish, or interactive channel. It does not prescribe the structure of a
homepage serving several audiences or turn ordinary product UI into a funnel.

## Establish the visitor's starting point

Use the brief and available product evidence to identify the offer, intended
audience, arrival source, prior knowledge, likely objections, and next action.
Separate known facts from assumptions. Ask only for missing information that
would materially change the page; otherwise state the assumption and proceed.
When visitors arrive from a specific campaign, preserve the promise they saw
and make the destination fulfill it. Different arrival contexts may need
different depth: a returning customer may need direct access, while an
unfamiliar product may need an explanation before the action makes sense.

## Build the argument around supported claims

For each consequential claim, identify its supporting evidence, where a visitor
will encounter it, and any material qualification. Keep the evidence close
enough to assess with the claim. A working demonstration can establish behavior;
a testimonial can report a person's experience; neither alone establishes a
general performance or conversion improvement.

Use real, attributable testimonials, customer identities, and measurements.
If proof is unavailable, narrow the claim, demonstrate what can be verified,
or record the gap for the owner. Illustrative content must be visibly identified;
never invent endorsements or precise-looking results to make a layout credible.
Only describe trials, refunds, guarantees, and cancellation terms the product
actually offers.

Let the important unanswered questions determine sections and order. Put a
material objection near the decision it affects; do not automatically bury it
in an FAQ. Choose a prominent next action appropriate to this page's purpose,
with wording that accurately describes what follows. Preserve useful alternate
paths when visitors have genuinely different needs. No fixed section count,
headline formula, animated tagline, font list, or universal single-CTA rule is
required. Keep the host's brand, accessibility, and motion contracts.

## Verify the path and the limits of the evidence

Trace arrival promise → page claim → adjacent evidence → action → destination.
Check that qualifications remain readable on narrow layouts and without motion,
and exercise the actual action, including any consequential terms shown before
commitment. Reconcile the finished copy with its sources. Record unsupported
claims as gaps rather than polishing them into apparent facts.

Rendering and path checks establish what the page displays and does. Treat
better comprehension or conversion as a hypothesis until supported by relevant
user research or measurement; do not present this recipe as a proven uplift.
